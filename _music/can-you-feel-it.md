## Listen first

Play it from Trax Records' official reissue, [The Complete "Can You Feel It"](https://bleep.com/release/129213-mrfingers-the-complete-can-you-feel-it) (previews on Bleep), or [Mr. Fingers on Apple Music](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/mr-fingers/56379534). The versions with speeches on top are later mash-ups (see below); start with the instrumental.

- Listen to the chords: Heard thinks he wrote them first.
- Listen to the bass: built "alongside" the chords.
- Listen to the kick: four on the floor, the template Heard took from disco.
- Listen for anything that sounds played by hand on top: that was the second cassette pass.

## The structure

No source gives section timings, so there is no timeline here. What Heard says about the shape: it was a jam ("you're just dabbling on the synthesizer until something comes to you") recorded in one pass, with a second pass of hand-played parts on top. The famous versions with a Martin Luther King speech or Chuck Roberts' "This is my house" are later mash-ups; of the Chuck Roberts one Heard says, "I had nothing to do with that one".

## How each part is made

### Only two machines

Heard: "I used a Roland Juno-60 and a TR-909 drum machine. That's all the gear used on the song." Roland's own history adds that he bought both "with some savings from his government job" after leaving his band in 1984.

### Chords first, then the bass

"'Can You Feel It?' would be another one where you're just dabbling on the synthesizer until something comes to you. Maybe I came up with chords and then constructed a bassline alongside that." Note the "maybe": he isn't certain, and we don't make it more certain than he does. The chords and the bass notes are not documented in our sources.

### The beat: a template from disco

Heard on his early tracks: "We used disco and the early electro music as our pattern, with the four-on-the-floor drum beat, and you just kind of wing the rest of it." Four on the floor means a kick on every beat: in a 16-step bar, steps 1, 5, 9 and 13. That is the only part of the drum pattern Heard describes.

```steps
Kick, four on the floor :: x...x...x...x...
```

### Two cassette decks

"I had two cassette decks—there were no digital recorders or even multi-track recorders—and I did one take, one pass, on one tape, then ran it back to the other one, played some other parts by hand that I wanted to add, and that was pretty much the recording process. It wasn't exactly the Beatles."

### The intent

Not a plan: "I don't recall specifically attempting to do anything" (he says this of "Mystery of Love", made the same way on his first day with the gear). For "Can You Feel It" his words are the "dabbling" quote above.

## Rebuild it on my rig

**This is our rebuild, not the original.** The PERfourMER stands in for the Juno-60 (chords on three channels, bass on the fourth), the Digitone II for the TR-909. Every control does what its manual says; the positions are our starting points. We copy Heard's *method* (two passes), not his notes, which are undocumented.

**Wiring the Digitone II in (once):** PERfourMER **MIDI THRU** → Digitone II **MIDI IN** (PERfourMER manual p. 23); on the Digitone, **SETTINGS › MIDI CONFIG › SYNC**: **CLOCK RECEIVE** and **TRANSPORT RECEIVE** on (manual p. 74).

## 1. Digitone II: the four-on-the-floor kick

1. Pick **FM DRUM** on a track: **[FUNC] + [SYN]**, choose it, **[YES]** (manual p. 89).
2. A round kick: a short **SDEP** pitch sweep (page 1) and a medium body **DEC** (page 3) (pp. 94–95). Tune by ear.
3. **[RECORD]**, then trigs on **[TRIG] 1, 5, 9 and 13** (grid recording, p. 43). Other drums by ear: Heard doesn't describe them.

## 2. PERfourMER: the chords (channels 1, 3, 4)

Channels **1, 3 and 4** on MIDI channel 1, PLAYMODE **P1** (manual p. 27). Set all three the same:

```settings
PLAYMODE :: [M1|M2|*P1*|P2|D1|D2] :: Three-voice chords.
WAVE :: [sine|triangle|square|*saw*|noise|EXT] :: Our choice; Heard's Juno-60 patch isn't documented.
OCTAVE :: 8' :: Middle register.
CUTOFF :: 10 o'clock :: Soft. Lower = more muffled (manual p. 12).
RESONANCE :: 9 o'clock :: Barely.
ATTACK :: 10 o'clock :: A soft start.
DECAY :: noon :: Gentle.
SUSTAIN :: 2 o'clock :: The chord holds while held.
RELEASE :: noon :: A short tail. With only three voices, keep release short so new chords don't cut old ones (manual p. 27).
PAN :: noon :: Into the pedals.
```

## 3. PERfourMER: the bass (channel 2)

The rig's bass patch, unchanged. Build the line on the OXI's sequencer 2 *after* the chords, as Heard did: play the chord loop and find bass notes that sit under it.

## 4. Grand Canyon and NightSky

Neutral (rig page). Heard names no effects, and the Juno-60's own effects aren't mentioned in our sources, so we add none. If you want space, a little NightSky **REVERB** is our choice, not his.

## 5. Record it like Heard

1. **Pass one:** the OXI plays the kick, the bass and the chords. Record it.
2. **Pass two:** play the recording back and add parts by hand on the PERfourMER (keys or OXI live input), recording the result. Heard: "played some other parts by hand that I wanted to add".

## What needs your ears

- The chords, their voicing, and the bassline: not documented. Roland's writer hears "notes ... elegantly clustered"; check that by ear.
- The drum parts beyond the kick.
- Whether the Juno-60's chorus is on: no source says.
