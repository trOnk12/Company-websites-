1. The kernel hands out memory in small blocks of the same size: pages. Zygote's ART and framework fill many of them.

  2. After a fork, the new processes don't get their own copies: they share Zygote's pages. Predict: what if your app changes one?

  3. Then, and only then, the kernel copies that one page for your app. The others keep the original: copy-on-write.

  4. Most pages are never changed, so they stay shared: twenty apps cost little more than one framework.
