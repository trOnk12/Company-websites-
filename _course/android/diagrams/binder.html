1. Your app calls a method that lives in system_server. In your process sits a proxy: an object that looks exactly like the one in system_server.

  2. Objects can't leave a process; bytes can. The proxy packs the arguments into a Parcel: a flat envelope of bytes.

  3. Binder carries the Parcel and stamps it with your app's UID, its key. Predict: who picks it up at the town hall?

  4. A courier: one of system_server's Binder threads, waiting for calls. It unpacks the Parcel and calls the real method.

  5. The reply travels back the same way, and the call in your app returns.

  6. It works both ways: system_server holds a proxy for your app too, and its calls, like "resume", arrive on your app's own Binder threads. Puzzle 14 follows one.

  7. Calls into system_server wait in its Binder buffer, a fixed-size area of its memory: about 1 MB, shared by every call in progress. An Intent can carry data, but a 3 MB photo doesn't fit: the call fails.
