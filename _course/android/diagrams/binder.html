1. Your main thread calls a method that lives in system_server. It looks local, because your process holds a proxy: an object with the same methods as the real one. Time goes down.

  2. Objects can't leave a process; bytes can. The proxy packs the arguments into a Parcel, a flat run of bytes, and calls transact(). From here your thread is blocked.

  3. The Binder driver in the kernel copies the bytes into system_server's memory and stamps them with your app's UID. Predict: who in system_server runs the call?

  4. One of system_server's Binder threads, which wait only for calls like this. In onTransact() it unpacks the Parcel, checks your UID, and runs the real method.

  5. The reply comes back the same way, and only now does your call return. The red bar is how long your main thread, and so your screen, waited.
