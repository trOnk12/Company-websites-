1. Your app calls a method that lives in system_server. In your process sits a proxy: an object that looks exactly like the one in system_server.

  2. Objects can't leave a process; bytes can. The proxy packs the arguments into a Parcel: a flat envelope of bytes (a byte is the smallest unit of data a program stores). Your thread that called now waits.

  3. Binder, in the kernel, carries the Parcel and stamps it with your app's UID, its key. Predict: who picks it up at the town hall?

  4. One of system_server's Binder threads: ordinary threads inside a process that wait for Binder to hand them calls. It unpacks the Parcel and calls the real method.

  5. The reply (a result, or just "done") travels back the same way, and only now does your waiting thread go on. If it was the main thread, the screen waited too.
