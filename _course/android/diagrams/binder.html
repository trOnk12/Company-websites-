1. Your app wants system_server to do something, but they're separate processes. Your code calls a proxy: a local object that looks exactly like the service.

  2. The proxy packs the method's arguments into a Parcel, a flat buffer of bytes. Objects can't cross processes; bytes can.

  3. The kernel's Binder driver copies the Parcel into system_server, and stamps it with your app's UID. The receiver can't be fooled about who's calling: that's what permission checks rely on.

  4. In system_server, one of its Binder threads wakes up. A stub unpacks the Parcel and calls the real method.

  5. The reply travels back the same way, and your proxy call returns.

  6. The catch: each process has a Binder buffer of about 1 MB, shared by all calls in flight. Put a 3 MB bitmap in an Intent and it doesn't fit: TransactionTooLargeException.
