1. This is your app: a process with your code in it and one main thread. Now notice what's missing: nothing in here decides when your screen starts, pauses or dies.

  2. That decision is made in another process, system_server. Think of it as the town hall: it keeps the official register of every app and every screen, and it decides "start screen X now".

  3. The town hall can't reach into your house: processes can't touch each other's memory. So the message goes through the kernel's Binder driver, the post office.

  4. The letter lands in your main thread's inbox. The main thread is one clerk who handles letters one at a time, in order.

  5. When the clerk reaches it, your callback runs: onCreate(). You didn't call it. You were called. Remember this chain; every puzzle in this course is one piece of it.
